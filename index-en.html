01 / PROJECT APPROACH

Validate the species first.
Then engineer the system.

Freshwater pearl culture cannot be designed by copying farms from other countries. Species biology, life cycle, available water and implantation response determine the system. In Yucatán, protection of the karst aquifer and escape prevention are core design constraints.

Contained experimental unit

Modular land-based facility with independent tanks, recirculation, water treatment, quarantine, observation and effluent control. No open hydraulic connection to cenotes, wetlands or natural water bodies.

Stage-gated research

Water characterization and taxonomic screening; husbandry and feeding trials; growth and condition assessment; only then, pearl implantation trials under specialist protocols.

Evidence-based design

Stocking density, flow, filtration capacity and operating parameters must be established through system testing and biological response—not copied from unrelated species or systems.

Start-up condition: no exotic species import, release or stocking is proposed. Before sourcing animals, verify taxonomy, lawful origin, health status, ecological risk and applicable authorizations. If no suitable and authorized species is identified, culture does not proceed.
02 / BIOLOGICAL BASIS

Freshwater pearl-producing
bivalves.

Freshwater pearls are mainly produced by unionid mussels. Pearl suitability is not universal across the group: it depends on species, anatomy, physiology, mantle condition, nucleation or implantation method and post-procedure survival.

Taxonomic identification

  • Expert identification supported by photographic and morphological records.
  • Molecular confirmation when morphology is inconclusive.
  • Documented source, origin population and permits.
  • Review of native range, endemism and conservation status.

Biological assessment

  • Candidate animal size and age range.
  • Body condition, filtration activity and handling response.
  • Host requirements and larval life cycle, where applicable.
  • Species suitability for captive propagation or lawful captive sourcing.
Species cited in international literature include Hyriopsis cumingii, Hyriopsis schlegelii and Cristaria plicata. These are literature references, not recommendations for introduction into Mexico. Their use would require ecological risk assessment and express authorization; native or already-authorized candidates take priority.
03 / AQUACULTURE ENGINEERING

Land-based recirculation
with isolatable circuits.

A modular controlled-flow facility is proposed. Recirculation can reduce make-up water demand and stabilize conditions, but does not eliminate purging, solids control, suitable feed or effluent management.

Receiving and quarantine

Separate tanks by batch, dedicated tools, behavior observation, health inspection, mortality records and movement controls. Quarantine water does not return to the main loop without validated treatment.

Husbandry area

Replicated experimental tanks, conservative trial densities, uniform flow, species-appropriate substrate or supports and access for inspection without excessive handling.

Water treatment

Pre-filtration and solids separation, biofiltration where loading requires it, aeration/oxygenation, temperature control and treatment of make-up or effluent water according to health assessment. UV does not replace filtration or biosecurity.

Hydraulics and redundancy

Pumps sized from measured flow and head loss; isolation valves per tank; level, flow and oxygen alarms; backup power and emergency aeration. Avoid a single point of failure shared by all batches.

Feeding and suspended particles

Bivalves are filter feeders. Diet depends on suitable organic particles and microalgae, particle size and concentration, filtration rate and renewal. Feed acceptance, ration and frequency require trials; a biofilter is not a food source.

Discharge and waste

Capture solids, segregate mortalities, and treat effluent according to risk and permits. Do not discharge cultured organisms, larvae, microalgae or untreated water into the environment.

04 / OPERATING PROTOCOL

From animal intake
to pearl assessment.

Husbandry and implantation must remain separate phases. Implantation should not begin until baseline survival, mantle condition and recovery capacity have been established in the selected species.

1 · Intake and verification2 · Quarantine3 · Acclimation4 · Husbandry and growth5 · Anatomical assessment6 · Authorized implant trial7 · Recovery and monitoring8 · Harvest and characterization

Before any implantation

  • Protocol developed and supervised by a specialist experienced in bivalve surgery.
  • Untreated control group and replicated experimental groups.
  • Record size, condition, handling time and immediate response.
  • Predefined stop criteria for mortality, stress or infection.

Post-procedure follow-up

  • Observe valve closure, filtration, feeding response and mortality.
  • Scheduled checks without unnecessary opening or handling.
  • Record implant loss/rejection and tissue condition.
  • Assess nacre, shape, surface and consistency at trial completion.

06 / BIOSECURITY AND ENVIRONMENT

A contained farm
in a karst landscape.

Aquifer protection and prevention of biological introductions are design criteria, not after-the-fact corrective measures. Yucatán cenotes and groundwater systems must not be used as open experimental units.

Physical containment

Drain screens and barriers, double containment for animal vessels, inventory and intake/dispatch counts, and escape or flood response procedures.

Health separation

Batch quarantine, dedicated tools, validated cleaning and disinfection, visitor controls and traceability for water, animals and biological materials.

Aquifer protection

Assess hydrogeology, subsurface vulnerability, water source, chemical storage and effluent treatment. Avoid soakaways or subsurface discharge without authorization and assessment.

Biosecurity rule: no mussel, larva, tissue, transport water or cultured microalgae may be released into cenotes, wetlands, rivers, mangroves or natural water bodies. Waste and effluent must follow an approved protocol.
07 / VALIDATION PLAN

Technical gates before
scaling up.

Progress depends on verifiable results. Biological timelines depend on species and must not be promised before the organism is identified.

Phase 0 · Pre-feasibility

Taxonomy, regulatory and literature review, lawful animal availability and preliminary water/site characterization.

Output: candidate species and site

Phase 1 · System without animals

Hydraulic tests, thermal stability, oxygenation, alarms, treatment, backup power, cleaning and effluent handling.

Output: stable and safe system

Phase 2 · Husbandry trial

Replicated trials for survival, feeding, condition, density and water quality; control groups and stop criteria.

Output: baseline husbandry protocol

Phase 3 · Pearl trial

Only with permits and specialist oversight: experimental procedure, untreated control, recovery follow-up, nacre formation and quality.

Output: demonstrated biological response

Phase 4 · Cycle validation

Repeat batches and seasons; confirm consistency in survival, growth, reproduction/sourcing and pearl outcome.

Output: repeatable evidence

Phase 5 · Scale engineering

Final sizing from measured biological load, water capacity, treatment, redundancy, permits, risks and environmental monitoring.

Output: validated detailed design
MIPEZ DESIGN BASIS

Biology defines engineering.

MIPEZ proposes freshwater pearl culture research in a contained, modular land-based facility in Yucatán. Species selection, life cycle, water quality, feeding and implantation response must be demonstrated before farm capacity is sized. The previous marine Pteria sterna concept for La Paz, Baja California Sur, is outside this technical proposal.
