02 / PRODUCTION MODEL

Two stations, one integrated culture workflow.

The 200-oyster pilot combines a land-based biological handling station with a marine grow-out and monitoring station. Each facility has a distinct role and they are connected through scheduled transfers and batch traceability records.

Land-based station · handling laboratory

A shore-side facility near the port for operations requiring hygiene, controlled conditions, precision equipment and individual handling.

  • Receiving, document checks, health inspection and quarantine.
  • Acclimation and temporary holding in seawater tanks.
  • Biometrics, selection, preparation and implantation by trained staff.
  • Initial post-operative observation and immediate recovery.
  • Harvest receiving, mabé extraction, cleaning, grading and traceability.
  • Sample analysis and temporary stock holding when needed.
Biological control and processing

Marine station · farm and field base

An operating area within the authorized marine site, supported by a work platform, vessel or floating structure; it is not a surgical theatre or implantation laboratory.

  • Longlines, moorings, buoys and culture baskets or lantern nets.
  • Batch transport, deployment and retrieval during scheduled visits.
  • Grow-out, post-implant recovery and nacre deposition at sea.
  • Monitoring of temperature, salinity, dissolved oxygen, turbidity and ocean conditions.
  • Biofouling removal and inspection of nets, moorings and mortality.
  • Batch-level growth, survival and condition sampling.
In-situ culture, maintenance and monitoring
Working species: Pteria sterna, subject to confirmation of lawful availability, origin, taxonomic identification, and validation with suppliers and authorities. The land station does not replace the sea as the grow-out environment; the marine station does not replace the laboratory for surgery, health control and processing.
03 / MARINE CULTURE SYSTEM

Suspended longline cultivation for pearl oysters.

The pilot uses marine longline culture with baskets or lantern nets. Land-based facilities are limited to receiving, quarantine, preparation and implantation; an intensive RAS is not proposed as the primary grow-out system.

1. Culture line and mooring

  • Mainline and dropper ropes sized after bathymetry and current surveys.
  • Floats, blocks or anchors selected for seabed type, wave exposure and design loads.
  • Baskets or lantern nets spaced for water exchange, cleaning and inspection.
  • Marking and placement compatible with navigation and authorized uses.

2. Oyster handling

  • Receive juveniles with supplier, batch, size and health-condition records.
  • Gradually acclimate stock to site conditions and record initial mortality.
  • Clean biofouling routinely and inspect nets, connections and flotation.
  • Sample each batch for growth, survival, condition and stress events.

3. Land-based support station

  • Hygienic area for receiving, grading, biometrics and implantation.
  • Temporary seawater holding tanks with aeration and basic mechanical filtration.
  • Backup pumping and power for handling contingencies.
  • Routine grow-out remains at sea; avoiding an intensive RAS reduces complexity and operating costs.

4. Validate before expansion

  • Confirm species and lawful stock origin with the relevant authorities and supplier.
  • Monitor temperature, salinity, dissolved oxygen, turbidity and chlorophyll through a representative cycle.
  • Estimate carrying capacity, maintenance frequency and actual site survival.
  • Expand only with documented results and current permits.
Design basis: final line, mooring, basket count and land-station sizing remain subject to oceanographic assessment, pilot testing and permits. Commercial stocking density and pearl yield are not presented as guaranteed figures.

04 / SITE ASSESSMENT & REGULATION

La Paz Bay & aquaculture
aquaculture permitting

Technical selection of marine polygons, oceanographic monitoring, and strict compliance with CONAPESCA, SEMARNAT, and CONANP prior to installation.

Site selection & critical variables

  • 1. Pre-select three polygons: review marine spatial planning, uses, access, navigation, and exclusion of sensitive areas (Espíritu Santo Archipelago National Park).
  • 2. Two stations per polygon: bathymetry, substrate type, currents, wave exposure, and storm protection.
  • 3. 8–12 week campaign + 12-month monitoring: temperature, salinity, dissolved oxygen, pH, turbidity, chlorophyll-a, and absence of pollutant discharges.
NOM-058-SAG/PESC/SEMARNAT-2013

Regulatory pathway & permits

  • Commercial Aquaculture Permit (CONAPESCA): up to 60 business days resolution with complete technical dossier (DOF Agreement Aug 26, 2025). Contact: administracion.acuicola@conapesca.gob.mx · Tel. 800 667 4022 (ext. 58572 / 58562).
  • Environmental Impact Assessment (SEMARNAT): Environmental Impact Statement (MIA) or preventive report depending on works and location.
  • Complementary clearances: federal maritime zone concession, maritime signaling, and aquatic health transport permits.
