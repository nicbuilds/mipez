1. Freshwater aquaculture model

Commercial freshwater pearl culture is historically associated with Asian unionid mussels such as Hyriopsis cumingii, H. schlegelii and Cristaria plicata. Their use elsewhere does not establish that they are suitable or legally admissible in Yucatán.

Critical decision: do not release, stock or import exotic mussels for commercial production. First complete taxonomic, sanitary, legal and ecological screening. Prefer a native or already-authorized species only if its biology and response to pearl implantation can be demonstrated. If no viable candidate exists, stop or redesign before scaling.

Contained pilot unit

Land-based modular system with separate quarantine and experimental tanks, recirculation, mechanical and biological filtration, aeration, temperature control, backup power, effluent treatment and biosecurity. No open connection to cenotes, wetlands or natural waters.

Water monitoring

Characterize source and availability; temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen, alkalinity, hardness, calcium, conductivity, solids, nutrients, chlorophyll and relevant contaminants. Track flow, water use and effluent quality.

Feeding and health

Filter-feeding bivalves depend on suspended particles and microalgae. Determine food availability and stocking density experimentally; monitor condition, filtration, mortality, parasites and fouling. Clean water alone is not a complete feed strategy.

2. Yucatán site strategy

Yucatán offers access to design talent, workshops and tourism markets, but its karst geology makes water sourcing and site selection critical. Cenotes and aquifers are connected and vulnerable systems—not available industrial ponds.

Farm location

Select a site with compatible land use, lawful sustainable water access, reliable power, treatment space and separation from sensitive ecosystems. A hydrogeological assessment and permits are prerequisites.

Initial configuration

Roofed modular facility: quarantine tanks, replicated experimental tanks, sump, filters, biofilter, UV as a supplementary barrier, basic lab and records. No discharge without characterization and authorization.

Design center

Keep manufacturing separate from the farm. Mérida can host design, prototyping, photography, packaging and sales; the aquaculture unit should be located where technical studies identify suitable conditions.

4. Two design systems, one brand

Full Pearl Construction

Pearls are the dominant visual structure: mini bags, clutches, mesh tops, corsets, belts and limited-edition pieces. Use modular assemblies and concealed supports to distribute weight and tension.

Pearl Fusion

Combine pearls with textiles, leather alternatives, denim, chains, steel and silver. Suitable for bags, wallets, tops and accessories across more price points.

5. Economics: validate before sizing

A retail necklace price divided by its pearl count is not a farm-gate pearl price. A MXN $4,000 necklace containing 75–100 pearls implies MXN $40–53 per pearl by simple division, but that price also pays for selection, assembly, clasp, brand, channel, taxes and retail margin.

The previous marine model does not transfer. The 50,000-oyster target, MXN $450 mabé assumption and marine CAPEX/OPEX are not freshwater production assumptions. A new model requires a lawful species, site, survival, culture time, saleable-pearl rate and verified quotations.

7. Preliminary regulatory and environmental pathway

Fisheries and aquaculture

Consult CONAPESCA and Yucatán authorities on research/farming versus commercial aquaculture, unit registration, stocking notices and production/harvest reporting.

Species and biosecurity

Verify identity, status, source, health, transport and import rules. Exotic introductions may require permits and risk assessment. Never move live organisms or water between basins without authorization.

Water and discharge

Determine CONAGUA requirements for water use, works and discharge based on source, volume, site and receiving environment. Design recirculation and treatment to minimize extraction and effluent.

Environmental review

Consult SEMARNAT on environmental impact assessment, land-use change and site-specific authorizations. Avoid cenotes, wetlands, mangroves and protected vegetation.

Aquatic health

Consult SENASICA on certificates, movement, quarantine, surveillance and sanitary measures applicable to live mollusks and aquaculture facilities.

Trade and claims

Describe products accurately as freshwater cultured pearls, with species and origin only when documented. Do not make unsupported sustainability or local-origin claims.

A recent Mexican review documents ecological risks associated with non-native freshwater mollusks. MIPEZ should prioritize containment, species diagnosis, traceability and escape prevention. The farm must not connect to cenotes or natural waters.

Investment principle

Fund knowledge and validation first—not a 50,000-animal farm. The first milestone is to prove that a legally cultivable species, a water-compatible Yucatán system and a profitable fashion product can coexist. In parallel, validate the design brand using traceable pearls from established suppliers.

Each aquaculture expansion requires documented survival, pearl quality, unit cost, biosecurity, permits and demand.
